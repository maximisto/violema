01The problem

54 electrons in 36 orbitals

The singlet (S = 0) state of an iron-sulfur cluster whose four iron centres are open-shell. The benchmark fixes its electronic problem in one public file, and its target is a variational upper bound, not the exact energy.

[Fe₄S₄(SCH₃)₄]²⁻ · schematic, not the pinned geometry, hydrogens omitted

  • Fe
  • S
  • C, methyl

Ms = 0 determinants, before spatial symmetry

8,862,957,169,158,400

C(36, 27)²: every way to place 27 spin-up and 27 spin-down electrons in 36 orbitals. The ground state is a weighted mix of a great many of them.

Singlet (S = 0) states among them

1,171,176,483,067,360

C(36, 27)² − C(36, 28)·C(36, 26). The target is a singlet, so the space a spin-adapted solver works in is about 7.6 times smaller. No solver enumerates either space: DMRG, selected configuration interaction and SQD each restrict the search differently. The size of the space is not evidence that a quantum computer is faster.

Largest subspace diagonalized on this model

1.0 × 10¹⁰

determinants, about one in 890,000 of the Ms = 0 space: the SQD run behind the tracker’s quantum entry (Shirakawa et al., 2025).

02Where the field stands

Posted energies on one Hamiltonian

Posted energies for this same Fe₄S₄ problem, measured above the reference: the lowest published variational bound on the pinned file. The 2017 model files list a DMRG value 7.4 mEh above it, and the tracker files this instance as a baseline benchmark with no advantage candidate. Lower is a tighter upper bound. The runs did not share a budget, and none is an attempt under this test. Choose Pass line to see the line up close.

Posted energies, CAS(54, 36)

Posted 2025-11-11 · quantum hardware

−326.913 Eh

IBM and RIKEN, sample-based quantum diagonalization on 77 qubits, first public as a preprint on 2025-10-31 (Shirakawa et al.), 278 mEh lower than the team’s 2024 result. As posted: 10 hours of quantum processor time and 10 hours on 76,032 Fugaku nodes; the submission and its preprint describe the two phases as overlapping. At IBM’s public pay-as-you-go rate, which starts at US$96 a minute, the quantum time alone would cost US$57,600 or more, over half this test’s US$100,000 ceiling per run, before any Fugaku time.

Posted 2025-11-20 · classical

−327.175 Eh

TrimCI (University of Wisconsin–Madison), a random-determinant method. As posted: the SQD energy in 15 minutes on a 20-core Apple M1 Ultra, and this one, 262 mEh lower, in 14 hours on 1,536 cores.

Posted 2026-01-08 · classical reference

−327.247036 Eh

Spin-adapted DMRG with entanglement-minimized orbitals, Zhai et al., on the pinned file: the lowest published variational bound on it, and zero on this chart. Later, GPU-accelerated DMRG (Legeza et al., 2026-03-30) reached −327.24466 Eh, 2.38 mEh above it, in about 12.6 hours on one DGX B200 node.

Pass line

≤ −327.245436 Eh

The reference plus 1.6 mEh (0.0016 Eh, about 1.004 kcal/mol). Any verified energy at or below it passes. Violema chose this absolute tolerance on this file; it is not a claim of experimental accuracy.

As posted, not rerun here. The tracker stores the same Hamiltonian in a different orbital basis, which leaves the energies unchanged, so its entries compare directly; Zhai et al. name the pinned file. Legeza et al. do not name their integral file: that they used the same Hamiltonian is inferred from agreement with results on the pinned file. 1 mEh is a thousandth of a hartree. Marks are not joined: no line implies what was posted between them. Runtimes are as each group reported them, not rerun; none follows this test’s ten-run protocol or budget accounting. The reference is set by rule: the lowest published, non-extrapolated variational upper bound for the S = 0 state on the pinned file, frozen when the test is registered. Lower bounds published after that enter the comparison and do not move the target. It stays provisional until registration confirms the spin sector and the file’s hash. Later entries may exist; this is the record as checked on 2026-10-24. The test itself is about time to reach the pass line.

03How fast the field moves

Ten years, in the order results became public

Select any mark to read it. Two arcs show how soon classical work was posted after a hardware result: once for a spin model in 2023, once for this Hamiltonian in 2025. The 180-day window exists so a later classical answer still counts.

Evidence time, 2016 to 2026

2025-10-31Hardware run

Shirakawa et al., IBM and RIKEN, 21 authors

arXiv 2511.00224 ↗

Closed-loop sample-based quantum diagonalization on IBM Heron processors with up to 152,064 nodes of the Fugaku supercomputer. For this [4Fe-4S] (54e, 36o) model it used 77 qubits and a 10¹⁰-determinant subspace, reaching −326.912 hartree, 278 mEh lower than the team’s 2024 result. The authors describe its accuracy as comparable to some all-classical approximation methods.

Dates are first public versions (preprint or announcement), not journal years. The cost estimates measure different things under different hardware assumptions, so they are placed in time, not plotted against each other. The axis changes scale at 2023. Each mark is an observation; in a full map it would be a claim with its own tier.
